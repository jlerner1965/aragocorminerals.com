The role

What carbonate media do in water systems.

Calcium carbonate media are used in water treatment for pH adjustment and acid neutralization, for remineralization of desalinated and reverse-osmosis water, and as filtration media. In a “calcite contactor,” acidified soft or desalinated water passes through a bed of carbonate granules; the carbonate dissolves, raising calcium hardness, carbonate alkalinity and pH to produce stable, non-corrosive water.

Non-corrosive buffering

Carbonate media are valued for being non-corrosive and self-buffering, avoiding the pH overshoot that lime and hydrated-lime dosing can risk.

Nutrient-water contexts

Aragonite is documented as effective in phosphate and phosphonate removal and in the remediation of nutrient-polluted water.

Why aragonite

More reactive by nature — and here that is the point.

Aragonite is more soluble and more reactive than calcite under comparable conditions. In a contactor whose entire function is the controlled dissolution of carbonate into water, higher reactivity is a direct process advantage, not a marginal one. It is the same property that counts against aragonite as a polymer filler — here it becomes the feature rather than the drawback.

Aragonite-specific finding

This reactivity advantage applies specifically to duties built around dissolving carbonate into water — contactor remineralization and pH adjustment. It is a statement about how the mineral behaves, not a claim that aragonite is universally better than calcite for every water application.

The honest framing

Two questions, kept separate.

Regulatory compliance is a separate question. Whether this material meets a given system’s potable-water requirements is not something we assert on your behalf; it must be confirmed for the target system with current documentation. We can provide what we have on request.

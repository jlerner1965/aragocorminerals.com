What it is

A biogenic calcium carbonate, sized for the field.

Oolitic aragonite is a naturally precipitated, ocean-derived form of calcium carbonate. Its grains are spherical ooids (roughly 50–500 µm) built from interlocking acicular crystals, giving high surface area and internal microporosity. It is the orthorhombic aragonite polymorph — distinct from the calcite that makes up quarried limestone. Representative composition is 97.8% CaCO3 with roughly 39–40% elemental calcium; it is mechanically collected, dried, screened and sized, with no chemical synthesis or high-temperature calcination.

Aragonite-specific finding

Faster calcium delivery

Aragonite is more soluble and reactive than calcite under comparable agronomic conditions, so calcium becomes available more readily.

Established industry practice

Liming value in acidic soils

As a carbonate, it supplies alkalinity and buffers acidic soils — a standard, uncontroversial liming function.

Why aragonite

Mineral form and particle architecture — not calcium percentage.

The defensible case for aragonite over calcitic limestone rests on how the mineral is built, not on how much calcium it carries. Both are essentially calcium carbonate; the difference is reactivity and surface behavior.

Higher surface area

Approximately 1.8 m2/g for oolitic aragonite versus about 0.55 m2/g for a calcitic-limestone benchmark. Representative, internal data — customer testing advised.

Aragonite-specific finding

More reactive by nature

The aragonite polymorph is more soluble than calcite under comparable conditions, which is what underlies faster calcium delivery.

Established industry practice

Low-dust handling

High microporosity with a free-flowing, uniform grain — low-dust and blend-friendly compared with dustier, higher-volume ground limestone.

The honest framing

A performance calcium source, not a commodity lime.

Where it fits. A higher-reactivity calcium carbonate for programs where speed of calcium delivery, blending performance and handling matter more than price per ton.

Not a universal lime replacement. Where price per ton dominates commodity liming, it competes directly with cheap quarried ag lime. We position it toward programs that value reactivity, blending and handling — not as a blanket substitute for ground limestone.

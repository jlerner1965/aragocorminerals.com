The role

What carbonate does in the glass batch.

Calcium carbonate is a standard raw material in the soda-lime glass batch — the source of calcium oxide (CaO), one of the three principal ingredients alongside silica sand and soda ash. On heating, the carbonate decomposes and releases CO2, leaving the oxide in the melt.

Why aragonite

It behaves distinctly — which is a reason to evaluate it.

In glass-batch pelletizing work, batches made with aragonite as the calcium source produced pellets of lower specific gravity (about 2.13 g/cm3) than calcite-source equivalents (about 2.17 g/cm3), with different crushing-strength behavior. It is a narrow but real, citable data point that aragonite behaves differently from calcite in batch preparation.

Aragonite-specific finding

This supports evaluating aragonite in batch preparation on its own merits — it is not a claim that aragonite makes better glass. That question is decided by the numbers in the next section, not by the polymorph alone.

What matters to a glass buyer

Iron content (Fe2O3)

Iron oxide is the impurity of greatest consequence in glass raw materials — it causes coloration, which matters most for clear/flint, optical and solar glass. This is the single most important number a glass buyer asks for. AragoCor does not publish an iron figure here and makes no “low iron” claim — request a per-lot trace-metal analysis for the material under evaluation.

The honest framing

Origin favors purity — iron decides fit.

Origin favors purity. The marine-biogenic origin of Bahamian oolitic aragonite means naturally high CaCO3 purity, which is favorable for glass.

Iron decides fit. Suitability for any specific glass type — container, float, fiber, solar or optical — turns on iron and trace-metal content that must be confirmed by current lot analysis. We can provide what we have on request; we do not assert a fit we have not measured for your grade.
